Length is not just a display setting.

Every visited cell pays its local squared cost. Adding penalty λ to each cell changes a path's total by λ × length, including both endpoints. Positive λ favors shorter paths. Negative λ rewards longer paths. This changes the objective, not the quality of an algorithm.

Conditioned on a fixed length, the penalty is constant across paths and cancels. Conditional occupancies and edge flows do not change with λ. Only the mixture weights change.

Same scalar bounds as the base inspector: lengths 1..7, samples -8..8, temperature 0.05..8. Penalty is -4..4. The cost direction embedded in the base request is retained for base verification, but does not affect length probabilities.

The penalty gradient equals expected length. Its curvature is -length variance / temperature, so expected length cannot increase when λ increases. Entropies are in nats. These are derivatives of the penalized raw soft-DTW score, not sequence accuracy metrics.

Entropy chain rule and numerical identities
Check the grouping, not just the total.
A length-resolved log-space dynamic program computes every legal stratum. Convolving prefix and suffix length messages gives conditional cell and edge probabilities. A separate verifier enumerates complete paths, groups them by visited-cell count and sums indicators directly. It also checks the penalized law directly over paths, independently of the mixture construction.
The base zero-penalty law is recovered by marginalizing length. Entropy separates into uncertainty about length plus average uncertainty within a length. For zero local costs, each conditional law is uniform, while the mixture still favors lengths with more paths.
Raw and penalized scores can be negative; neither is a metric. This is a bounded scalar synthetic experiment, not a learned model, benchmark, novel algorithm or self-corrected divergence. Floating-point rounding and probability underflow remain visible limitations.
